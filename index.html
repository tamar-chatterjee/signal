Session

Flicker safety. Screen flicker between roughly 3 and 60 Hz can trigger seizures in people with photosensitive epilepsy, including people who don't know they have it. Risk peaks around 15–25 Hz. Skip flicker mode if you or anyone nearby is photosensitive, and stop if you feel unwell. Steady mode has no such risk.

What this emits

Tone — a sine wave at the frequency shown. Note that phone speakers roll off hard below about 500 Hz, so the low presets will mostly produce harmonics rather than the fundamental you asked for.

Screen — the display fills with the selected colour, either steady or alternating with black. Total output is roughly 0.1 watts of light, comparable to the rear torch but spread across the whole panel rather than concentrated in a beam.

On wavelength — the slider is honest about one thing and approximate about another. The three primaries are real: the screen's red, green and blue emitters have genuine peaks near 630, 530 and 450 nm. Everything in between is a mix of those three that your eye reads as the named wavelength. A spectrometer pointed at 580 nm on this slider would see red and green peaks, not a single yellow one. That's a limit of every RGB display, not of this page.

On the water — nothing measurable. Visible light sits in the band where water is most transparent; roughly 0.2% of it is absorbed passing through a glass, and what is absorbed becomes heat, on the order of ten millionths of a degree. This is a timer with a tone and a colour. The useful part is that it makes you stop and drink the glass.
